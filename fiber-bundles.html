Fiber Bundles
an interactive atlas

A fiber bundle is a space $E$ that locally looks like a product but may be globally twisted. Formally, it is a surjection $\pi:E\to B$ such that every point of the base $B$ has a neighborhood $U$ with a homeomorphism

$\pi^{-1}(U)\;\cong\;U\times F$

commuting with projection to $U$. The preimage $\pi^{-1}(b)\cong F$ of a point is the fiber over $b$. Every bundle is assembled from trivial pieces $U\times F$; the interesting data is how the pieces are glued — the transition functions, valued in a structure group $G$ acting on $F$.

The bundle $S^1 \hookrightarrow S^3 \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } S^2$

The Hopf map sends $(z_1,z_2)\in S^3\subset\mathbb{C}^2$ to $[z_1:z_2]\in\mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2$. The fiber over each base point is a circle — the $U(1)$-orbit $(e^{it}z_1,\,e^{it}z_2)$ — and $S^3$ is the total space of a nontrivial principal $U(1)$-bundle: $S^3\not\cong S^2\times S^1$ (compare $\pi_1$).

What you see is $S^3$ stereographically projected to $\mathbb{R}^3$. Each circle is one fiber; every two fibers are linked (Hopf link, linking number $=$ the Euler class, $1$). Fibers over a circle of latitude sweep out a torus.

Paint the base. Click or drag on the small sphere (bottom right) — that sphere is the base $S^2$. Each point you touch grows its fiber in the big view. Hue encodes longitude, brightness encodes latitude.

Interval bundles over $S^1$

Glue $[0,2\pi]\times[-1,1]$ end-to-end with $k$ half-twists. For $k=0$ this is the trivial bundle — a cylinder, $S^1\times[-1,1]$. For $k=1$ it is the Möbius band: still locally a product everywhere, but globally twisted. Transition functions live in $G=\{\pm 1\}$, and bundles are classified by $k \bmod 2 \in H^1(S^1;\mathbb{Z}/2)$.

Transport the arrow. Press Transport around loop: an orientation of the fiber is carried once around the base circle. When $k$ is odd it comes back reversed — the visual proof that no continuous orientation (no nonvanishing section of the associated line bundle) exists.
